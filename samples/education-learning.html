Helping Students Learn Alongside AI

By Ezzah Chaudary · 1 min read

The question for schools is not only how students can access AI, but how they can use it while still practicing the skills learning is meant to develop.

Portfolio sample: This original demonstration is not represented as previously published client work.

Make the thinking visible

When a student submits a polished answer, it may be difficult to tell which parts they understand. Teachers can make the learning process visible by asking students to explain their choices, show a first draft, discuss a source, or reflect on what changed after feedback.

Give AI a limited, purposeful role

Students can use an AI tool to generate a quiz after studying a chapter, request another explanation, or receive feedback on an argument. The learner should still attempt the task and check the output. Asking for questions to consider can be more useful than asking for a complete essay.

Teach verification as a core skill

AI-generated material can be incomplete or incorrect. Students should practice checking claims against reliable sources, following citations to their originals, and comparing explanations. A confident tone is not proof of accuracy.

Set clear and fair expectations

Schools and educators should explain which uses are allowed for each task and when students must disclose assistance. Access matters too: not every student has the same device, connectivity, or paid tools.

Keep the purpose of education in view

Technology can support learning, but the goal is broader than producing a correct answer quickly. Students need judgment, curiosity, communication, and the ability to work through uncertainty. The measure of success is whether students become more capable of understanding and applying what they learn.
