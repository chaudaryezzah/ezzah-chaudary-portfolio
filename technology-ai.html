How to Use AI Without Outsourcing Your Thinking

By Ezzah Chaudary · 1 min read

AI tools can make difficult tasks feel easier. The real skill is knowing when to use them—and when to work through a problem yourself.

Portfolio sample: This original demonstration is not represented as previously published client work.

Start with your own attempt

When a task feels difficult, it is tempting to ask an AI assistant for an answer immediately. A more useful habit is to spend a few minutes forming your own view first. Write down what you understand, what confuses you, and what you think a good answer might include. This first attempt gives you something to compare with the tool’s response.

Use AI as a conversation partner

Instead of asking only for a finished product, ask questions that keep you involved. Ask for counterarguments, a practice problem, or feedback on your reasoning. You still have to decide which ideas are relevant, which evidence is reliable, and what conclusion makes sense.

Check the answer, not just the wording

Fluent writing can make an inaccurate answer seem credible. AI systems may misunderstand a question or present an unsupported claim as fact. Treat outputs as a starting point: verify important claims against credible sources and follow references to their original material.

Keep some productive friction

Not every difficult step needs to be automated. Drafting a paragraph, solving a practice problem, or explaining an idea from memory can feel slower than generating an answer instantly. Yet those efforts help build fluency and make knowledge easier to retrieve later.

Build a habit, not a prohibition

There is no single rule that fits every person or task. Try working independently before asking for help, use the tool to test your understanding, and reflect on whether you could explain the final result in your own words. The goal is not to avoid assistance. It is to remain an active participant in the work.
